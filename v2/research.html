What the final moments of a merger can tell us.

My work sits where general relativity meets public gravitational-wave data. The central project is a computational study of Kerr ringdown predictions for catalog events. A second thread applies the same measurement discipline to lunar-dust instrumentation.

Why Ringdown?

After two black holes merge, the new black hole rings as it settles into a quiet Kerr state, radiating gravitational waves. The frequencies and damping times of that signal are set by the remnant's mass and spin.

Guiding principle. A prediction is not a measurement. Every result in this project keeps the two clearly separate.

How the Project Works

OngoingIndependent computational research, October 2026 – present

  1. Select events from the public catalog.
  2. Calculate quasinormal-mode frequencies and damping times with the qnm package.
  3. Convert source frame to detector frame using catalog redshifts.
  4. Document inputs, assumptions, methods and results on GitHub.
